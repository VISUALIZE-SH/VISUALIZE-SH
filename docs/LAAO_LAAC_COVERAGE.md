# LAAO/LAAC device coverage

This is the article-to-catalog checklist for the AF condition in Data and Atlas. The catalog is an evidence index, not a statement that a historical device is currently sold or clinically available. Every new version is `draft` and source dated. Quantitative dimensions, sheath sizes, and treatment results remain absent unless a specific primary source supports them.

The [May–June 2018 CI Today spectrum](https://citoday.com/articles/2018-may-june/the-spectrum-of-devices-for-percutaneous-left-atrial-appendage-occlusion) supplies the historical inventory. [PMC11526496](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11526496/) adds the later device landscape. These review articles guide coverage only; original investigations, manufacturer specifications, FDA records, and field notices support substantive catalog facts. The first article's figure includes PLAATO, so PLAATO belongs in the checklist even though it receives little running text. Its early WATCHMAN FLX is the CE-marked 2015 iteration withdrawn in 2016; the redesigned WATCHMAN FLX received FDA approval in 2020 and retains a separate version record.

The endocardial `laao-occluders` category is labeled **LAA occlusion / closure (LAAO/LAAC)** in Data. LARIAT, Sierra, Cardioblate Closure, AtriClip, and Ecliptis close or exclude the appendage by epicardial ligation or surgical clipping and belong in the separate `laa-exclusion-ligation` category. Their delivery routes and dimensions must not be compared as though they were transseptal occluders.

The unresolved “Sealer” request has no version ID until a distinct manufacturer and device can be verified. SeaLA is a separately named LAA device. Historical approvals, CE marks, field actions, and trial records are source-date facts; none establish current commercial availability. Missing quantitative specifications should remain blank or explicitly unavailable, not inherited from a later generation.
