## What the valve evidence supports

MiRus identifies an investigational balloon-expandable aortic valve with a
molybdenum–rhenium frame and dry porcine leaflets. The product page describes the
alloy as nickel/cobalt-free, but does not establish exact elemental percentages.
Its performance comparisons rely on data on file; those comparisons were not
added as independently validated outcomes. [MiRus product document](https://www.mirusmed.com/solutions/structural-heart/siegel-transcatheter-aortic-valve-2/),
undated; locators: title, Molybdenum-Rhenium, Exceptional Biocompatibility and
comparative-testing footnote; retrieved October 14.

The May 2026 sponsor description adds a nitric oxide frame coating, balloon
premounting and 23/26/29-mm sizes using an 8 Fr expandable sheath. This is a nominal
profile, not maximum expanded diameter or a proven reduction in vascular injury.
The same disclosure calls the system investigational and not commercially approved
in any country **at that disclosure date**. It mentions unnamed mitral/tricuspid
replacement assets but supplies no names, materials or clinical configurations.
No pulmonary candidate was established in the bounded search.
[Boston Scientific release](https://news.bostonscientific.com/2026-05-18-Boston-Scientific-announces-strategic-investment-in-MiRus-LLC),
May 18, 2026; non-quotation design/sizing paragraphs, conditional asset-option
paragraph and caution; retrieved October 14. Unnamed assets are held as research
context, without new product/material records.

The transaction is an investment agreement and conditional acquisition option,
not proof that MiRus or its valve business has been acquired. The filing describes
IDE permission to conduct STAR in pursuit of a PMA; no commercial authorization
decision was added. [Boston Scientific Form 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885725/000088572526000041/bsx-20260518.htm),
May 18, 2026, Item 8.01; retrieved October 14.

## Other structural heart devices: answer and uncertainty

**No additional current device was confirmed from exact-product primary
documentation. A historical Beall mitral-valve use is reported in a scholarly
review and remains a verification lead.** Ribeiro et al.'s 2016 review,
*A perspective on molybdenum biocompatibility and antimicrobial activity for
applications in implants*, says Mo/Re preforms were coated for Beall valve struts.
The actual review's “Molybdenum in medical devices” section was inspected via
[Gale's public document](https://go.gale.com/ps/i.do?asid=17451253&id=GALE%7CA442893037&it=r&p=AONE&u=googlescholar&v=2.1).
Its reference 41 is Ratner et al., *Biomaterials Science* (2012). The cited book's
exact passage and Beall model/composition were not verified. Therefore no Beall
material claim was added to the device catalog, and no “first/only” claim is made.

The [1988 Beall model 105 report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3411991/)
abstract describes carbon-coated struts/wire fatigue but does not identify the
wire alloy. It neither confirms nor disproves MoRe use. Coronary stent patents
and vascular-wire research are separate from structural heart products.
[WO2004022122A2](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2004022122A2/en) describes
Mo/Re medical-device concepts, without establishing a named marketed product or
a public Siegel-to-patent connection; it was not added as a Siegel patent.

Coverage included Siegel/MiRus sources, ClinicalTrials.gov, PubMed, valve/mitral/LAA
material searches, vascular scaffolds, orthopedic research and historical Beall
leads. Two focused research agents supplied inventories; the coordinator checked
material sources, registry pages and original result slides. This is a bounded
documentary review, not a systematic review or an exhaustive regulatory search.
Podcasts, interviews, recordings and transcripts were excluded throughout.
