Acoramidis Shows Real-World Benefit Versus Tafamidis in ATTR-CM

A peer-reviewed U.S. claims-data study comparing acoramidis (170 patients) with tafamidis (448 patients) in ATTR-CM found a 34% lower risk of a composite clinical-worsening endpoint (diuretic intensification, HF hospitalization, or death) with acoramidis, and a 43% lower risk of diuretic intensification alone. BridgeBio, which markets acoramidis, sponsored the analysis and cautioned that administrative claims data capture cardiac biomarkers poorly and that the 4.6-month average follow-up is short given acoramidis' recent approval.

Sources: BridgeBio Pharma

2026-09-22

Corcym reports first-in-human implant of Memo 4D Curve, an open-configuration mitral annuloplasty ring

Corcym announced the first human implant of Memo 4D Curve, an FDA-cleared open-configuration addition to its Nitinol Memo mitral annuloplasty family, sized up to 42 mm for annuli ranging from standard degenerative disease to severely dilated. Michael Ibrahim performed the procedure at UVA Health; Corcym said it will formally introduce the ring internationally at the EACTS meeting in October 2026. No comparative outcomes data versus the existing closed Memo 4D ring were disclosed.

Sources: Corcym (BusinessWire release)

UBC's Heart Valve Performance Laboratory reports on a soft-closure mechanical valve prototype

The University of British Columbia's Heart Valve Performance Laboratory described the design rationale and pulse-duplicator hydrodynamic testing of iValve, a bileaflet mechanical heart-valve platform built around curved leaflets and distributed, progressive closure contact intended to reduce the rapid-closure flow events linked to mechanical-valve thromboembolism and hemolysis. Prototypes maintained large projected opening areas across 10 test cycles at physiologic flow and pressure conditions. # needs-fulltext: abstract/metadata only, full text is paywalled.

2026-09-18

Single-center study links TAVR performed more than 6 months after AS diagnosis to worse outcomes

A retrospective single-center study of 149 severe aortic stenosis patients (2021-2023) found the composite of heart-failure hospitalization or death occurred more often when TAVR was performed more than 6 months after diagnosis (n=49) than within 6 months (n=100), over a median 729-day follow-up. Guidelines already call for surveillance within roughly 6-12 months for patients who do not meet immediate-intervention criteria; the authors say the findings support timelier evaluation but call for larger, multicenter confirmation given the small, single-site, non-randomized design.

Sources: Open Heart
